Autonomous Discovery Lab

A closed-loop simulation of an AI scientist: plan, run, analyse, verify, audit, replan. You can see how capability, test-time compute, verification and risk policy change what gets claimed as a discovery.

Interpretation boundary. The campaign, landscapes and claims are simulated with a seeded generator. The proof panel is Lean-inspired and is not a real Lean runtime: no Lean kernel runs here. The only real measurements are METR's published time horizons (fetched 2026-11-12). The paper figures in Sources are quoted from local PDFs. "Promoted" means a claim passed this simulation's gates; it does not mean the claim is true. The ground-truth view exists only because this is a simulation.

Sets the modeled task horizon, so how often a run finishes. Also sets how often planning drifts and how sharp the judge is.
Analysis drafts N interpretations and a verifier picks one. Costs 0.01 × N units per analysis.
Chance that a hit is replicated (it confirms when the replicate reaches 0.80 − noise σ), and how good the verifier is. In the Lean-gated scenario the kernel checks every candidate exactly.
1 unit per experiment, 1 per replicate, 0.5 per kernel check. Applies on Reset.
A claim is promoted when its confidence ≥ 1 − tolerance. A verified claim has 0.95; an unverified one has 0.60.
UCB acquisition = predicted value + κ × uncertainty (active learning). Cells within 4 of a promoted or refuted claim are down-weighted, so the agent moves on to new regions.
The agent starts from a blurred, noisy map, as recipes proposed by literature-trained models would give it. The random planner never gets it. Applies on Reset.
